## A project is not a document

> “What Comes Before a Project?” grows out of a criticism I have been making for some time. People treat a project as if it were merely the production of a document. A project is not a cover page, table of contents, introduction, chapter one, chapter two, development, conclusion, and references. Those are documents or parts of documents; they are not the project.

> Before a specification or formal document, there is work that forms the object: understanding what is intended, why it should be done, which terms are being used, which criteria apply, which uncertainties remain, and what will count as a result. People confuse all of that or simply fail to perform a pre-project stage, a meta-analysis, or even an analysis of the frame used by the analysis itself.

> What I want to bring forward is that preceding layer. You managed to bring it forward, but then you began mixing different levels as if they were one thing, and the text became epistemologically incongruent.

> The concern is with the lifecycle. When people discuss product lifecycle management, attention often shifts toward user interface, user experience, presentation, and visibility, while engineering criteria become secondary. It seems that whoever makes a better PowerPoint than an engineer receives all the attention.

> In integrated circuits, this becomes obvious because the risk is very high and the propagation of an error can generate enormous losses. Chip projects need to be extremely organized. A small detail can multiply catastrophically. A project cannot be reduced to the document describing one part of the process.

> The problem is not exclusive to AI. A project can be poorly written or poorly formed by an AI, an intern, a programmer, or an engineer. The problem is that people do not read what precedes and organizes the stage in which they are working. Then they treat the template as if it were the project.
