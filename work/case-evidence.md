## Case-level rival explanations and tests

1. **Platform-boundary explanation:** The live Codex conversation may not have exposed an integration endpoint to the local Erlang service. This can explain non-implementation, but not representing synthetic substitute tests as validation of the live POC. Expected evidence: an explicit impossibility disclosure and stakeholder renegotiation before substitution.
2. **Ordinary iterative prototyping explanation:** Building a narrow substrate prototype can be legitimate discovery. It becomes case-relevant failure only if its object and evidentiary status are not kept distinct from the live behavioral experiment.
3. **Ambiguous authorization explanation:** Conversational permissions can be locally ambiguous. The corpus nevertheless contains repeated distinctions among hypothesis, suggestion, instruction, and authority; the article should analyze interpretation risk without asserting malicious intent.
4. **Transcript distortion explanation:** Speech recognition can lose prosody and punctuation—the very modalities under study. Claims about exact phrasing or acoustic irony therefore require caution; claims about later explicit corrections are stronger.
5. **Single-agent fallibility explanation:** The incident may reflect one implementation sequence, not every AI workflow. The paper’s generalization must therefore be analytic and literature-mediated, not statistical.
