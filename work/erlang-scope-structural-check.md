# Erlang/BEAM Scope Structural Check

Read-only audit of `outputs/before-the-project.tex` against the governing scope in `work/editorial-review-notes.md`.

- Manuscript snapshot: SHA-256 `9f6613fed7b485c7812d52713b5964f5cc7c4c903db6c79ba8dc01c6539eb230`
- Line references below refer to that snapshot.
- Governing distinction: the paper's transferable object is the neglected epistemic work that precedes and conditions any project, against reducing a project to a specification, plan, or document. Its first-person basis is a selective synthesis of roughly twelve months of personal engineering work probing AI capabilities, feasibility, limits, failure modes, useful boundaries, and possible improvements. These two levels must remain connected but distinct. The recent Erlang/BEAM Context Runtime is the latest uncertain trial, not a confirmed solution, truth model, universal oracle, final architecture, or explanation of all earlier work.

## Overall finding

The manuscript never simply declares Erlang to be the solution. It repeatedly calls the work an experiment, separates substrate from semantics, and limits the local tests. Those are substantive safeguards. The remaining problem is structural: the recent strand supplies almost all concrete design-trace material, occupies the last full technical section before the Discussion, and receives most of the appendical trace. In combination with declarative definitions and a single prescribed acceptance run, that placement makes an uncertain late trial function as the narrative culmination even where individual sentences are hedged.

## Claim-role separation map

The manuscript is strongest when it names the role of a statement. The following labels should remain inspectable wherever Erlang/BEAM material meets the bibliography:

| Role | Current example | Scope judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Source-supported statement | Available context need not be effectively used (`358`, citing “Lost in the Middle”). | Supported only at the retrieval-study level. |
| Engineering synthesis | A workflow should keep semantic, authority, and assurance loops visible (`211`). | Plausible proposal; not established by the local episode alone. |
| Analogy | Leveson’s component/system distinction applied to an Erlang supervisor (`258`). | Properly bounded and explicit. |
| Hypothesis | A conversational-runtime architecture may help preserve significance in wider scientific work (`148`). | Currently written too much like a demonstrated transfer. |
| Proposed terminology | “Anti-specification” (`128`) and “authority uncertainty” (`232`). | Clearly labeled; good model. “Experience Base” at `435-437` needs the same treatment. |
| Local experimental/appraisal result | Blind/guided review, T13/T14, and synthetic A/B slice (`419-425`, `550-562`). | Properly tied to local records and carefully bounded. |

## Bottom line

The manuscript already contains the right epistemic cautions; the revision need not erase the Erlang material. It needs to stop allowing an uncertain latest trial to occupy three roles at once: illustrative case, empirical warrant for the general framework, and final implementation. The transferable thesis should be argued at its own level; the first-person synthesis should show, thematically rather than artifact by artifact, how selected trials informed it. Erlang's safe role is narrower and still valuable: **one late, unfinished probe that makes selected recurring problems observable and supplies bounded local tests of a few proposed distinctions**.
