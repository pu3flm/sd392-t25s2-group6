## Verified conceptual content

### Terminology as an epistemic boundary

An unfamiliar term is not merely a vocabulary inconvenience. It marks a boundary in the current model of the domain. The disciplined response is inquiry: identify what the term denotes, how the speaker uses it, and what distinctions would be lost by replacing it with a familiar approximation.

### Terminology as an engineering interface

Terminology functions like an interface or type system between participants, requirements, architecture, implementation, and verification. An unexamined substitution can propagate semantic drift through every downstream stage while leaving the resulting artifact internally consistent and operationally wrong.

### Artifact validity and model validity

Machine-checkable closure is a property of an artifact. Correct understanding is a property of the model that produced or interpreted it. A document can compile, a program can run, and a test suite can pass without establishing that the governing intention was understood correctly.

### Silence is not coverage

The absence of objection is an observation, not evidence that the relevant semantic space was covered. Agreement cannot be inferred merely from continuation when terminology, authority, or criteria remain unresolved.

### Engineer, programmer, and accountability

The conversation distinguishes programming work from the socially entrusted role of the engineer. Corporate titles can blur that distinction, but the relevant criterion is not the label. It is whether the person exercises terminal technical judgment and remains accountable for the consequences of that judgment.

### Authority and indivisible responsibility

Engineering autonomy is paired with accountability. The engineer may use tools, models, assistants, and generated candidate artifacts, but delegated production does not delegate the final obligation to verify, approve, and underwrite the decision.

### AI-assisted drafting

An AI system can generate and refine candidate material. The accountable engineer determines whether that material correctly expresses the domain, intention, constraints, and evidentiary limits. Fluency cannot substitute for that judgment.
